## A defence system that is switched off until you cut

An intact onion doesn't make you cry. Its cells store sulfur-containing compounds and the enzymes that act on them in separate compartments. Slicing the onion breaks the cells open and lets them mix. Scientists think this is a chemical defence against animals that bite into the bulb.

## The chain reaction

1. An enzyme called **alliinase** turns the stored sulfur compounds into sulfenic acids.
2. A second enzyme, **lachrymatory factor synthase** (discovered by Japanese researchers in 2002), turns one of those acids into **syn-propanethial-S-oxide**.
3. That compound is volatile, so it drifts up as a gas toward your face.

## Why your eyes react

When the gas reaches your eyes, it stimulates sensory nerves in the cornea. Your body responds the way it would to any irritant: the tear glands release tears to wash it away.

## Tips that actually help

- Chill the onion first: cold slows down the reactions and makes the gas less volatile.
- Use a sharp knife: cleaner cuts crush fewer cells.
- Cut the root end last: that area is especially rich in the compounds involved.
