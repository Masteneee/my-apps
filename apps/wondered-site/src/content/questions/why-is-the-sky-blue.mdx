## Sunlight is a mix of colours

Sunlight looks white, but it contains every colour of the rainbow. Each colour is light with a different wavelength: violet and blue are short (around 400 to 490 nanometres), red is long (around 620 to 700 nanometres).

## Tiny molecules scatter blue light the most

Air is mostly nitrogen and oxygen molecules, which are far smaller than the wavelength of visible light. When light hits particles that small, it gets bounced off in random directions. This is called **Rayleigh scattering**, and its strength goes as 1 divided by the wavelength to the fourth power.

That fourth power matters a lot. Blue light at about 450 nm has a shorter wavelength than red light at about 700 nm, so it is scattered roughly (700 / 450)⁴ ≈ 5.9 times more. So wherever you look in the sky, scattered blue light is coming toward you.

## Then why isn't the sky violet?

Violet scatters even more than blue, but three things work against it: the Sun emits less violet than blue, some violet is absorbed high in the atmosphere, and our eyes are much less sensitive to violet. The mix our eyes actually receive looks sky blue.

## Bonus: red sunsets

At sunset, sunlight crosses much more air to reach you. Most of the blue gets scattered away along the path, so the light that arrives directly from the Sun is dominated by orange and red.
