## Floating is about density

An object floats when it is less dense than the liquid around it. Most substances get *denser* when they freeze, because their molecules pack closer together. Water is a strange exception.

## Hydrogen bonds build an open cage

A water molecule (H₂O) is bent, with a slightly negative oxygen and slightly positive hydrogens. The hydrogen of one molecule is attracted to the oxygen of a neighbour: that attraction is a **hydrogen bond**.

In liquid water these bonds constantly break and re-form, so molecules can crowd close together. When water freezes into ordinary ice (called ice Ih), each molecule locks into hydrogen bonds with four neighbours, forming a hexagonal crystal with lots of empty space inside.

## The numbers

- Liquid water near 0 °C: about 1.00 g/cm³
- Ice at 0 °C: about 0.917 g/cm³

Ice is roughly 9% less dense, which is why about 90% of an iceberg sits below the surface and only about 10% sticks out.

## Why it matters

Because ice floats, lakes freeze from the top down. The ice layer insulates the water below, so fish and other life can survive the winter underneath.
